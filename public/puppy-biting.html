Puppy biting

How to stop puppy biting in 3 simple steps

Those tiny teeth hurt. The good news: biting is normal puppy behavior, and with a few calm, consistent habits, most puppies learn to be gentle.

By Happy Dog Training TipsUpdated November 20266 min read

The short version

  • Puppies bite to explore, play and soothe sore gums. It's normal, not aggression.
  • Give them something better to bite, and make the fun pause when teeth touch skin.
  • Never hit or yell. It can scare your puppy and make biting worse.

Why puppies bite

Puppies explore the world with their mouths, the way babies use their hands. In a litter, they learn how hard is too hard by playing with their brothers and sisters. When a puppy comes home, that lesson isn't finished yet, so your hands become the new chew toy.

Teething makes it stronger. Between roughly 3 and 6 months old, adult teeth are coming in, gums are sore, and chewing feels good. A tired or overexcited puppy also bites more, just like a toddler gets cranky past bedtime.

The 3 steps

  1. Swap hands for toys

    Keep a soft chew toy within reach in every room. When your puppy goes for your hand, calmly offer the toy instead. The moment it chews the toy, praise it in a warm, happy voice. You're teaching a simple rule: toys yes, skin no.

  2. Pause the fun

    If teeth land on skin again, say a calm “oops,” stop moving and take your hands away for a few seconds. If it keeps going, step away or behind a baby gate for 20 to 30 seconds. Puppies love attention, so they learn fast that biting makes the fun stop.

  3. Reward calm moments

    Catch your puppy being gentle: licking your hand, lying quietly, chewing its own toy. Reward that with a small treat or soft praise. What gets rewarded gets repeated, so you'll start to see more of it.

Watch the clockA lot of biting happens when a puppy is overtired. Most young puppies need 16 to 18 hours of sleep a day. If biting gets wild, a quiet nap in a crate or pen often fixes it better than any trick.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Hitting, tapping the nose or yelling. It can make your puppy afraid of hands and lead to more biting, not less.
  • Playing with bare hands. Wrestling with your hands teaches that hands are toys. Use rope toys and tug toys instead.
  • Being inconsistent. If one person allows nibbling and another doesn't, your puppy gets mixed messages. Agree on the rules as a family.
  • Pulling your hand away fast. Quick movements look like prey and make the game more exciting. Go still instead.

Common questions

When do puppies stop biting?
Most puppies bite much less by around 6 to 7 months old, once adult teeth are in, especially if you've been teaching gentle mouths from the start. Every puppy is different, so stay consistent.
Is my puppy being aggressive?
Usually not. Play biting comes with a wiggly body, play bows and a relaxed face. If you see stiff freezing, growling with a hard stare, or biting when guarding food or toys, talk to a vet or a certified trainer.
Should I yelp like a puppy when it bites?
It works for some puppies and makes others more excited. If yelping winds your puppy up, switch to a calm “oops” and a short pause instead.
