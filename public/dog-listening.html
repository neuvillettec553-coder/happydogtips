Not listening

Teach your dog to listen in 10 minutes a day

Your dog isn't ignoring you to be stubborn. Usually, it just hasn't learned that paying attention to you is the best thing that can happen.

By Happy Dog Training TipsUpdated November 20265 min read

The short version

  • Listening starts with attention. Teach your dog to look at you first.
  • Short sessions of 3 to 5 minutes, two or three times a day, beat one long session.
  • Practice in a quiet room first, then slowly add distractions.

Why dogs “don't listen”

When a dog ignores a cue, it's rarely about respect. Most of the time, one of three things is happening: the dog doesn't fully understand the word yet, something else is more interesting, or listening hasn't been worth it in the past.

That's why the fix isn't a louder voice. It's teaching your dog that checking in with you pays off, then practicing in places that slowly get more distracting.

The 10-minute plan

Split these 10 minutes into two or three short sessions during the day. Use tiny, soft treats your dog loves.

  1. Teach “look”

    Say your dog's name once in a happy voice. The moment it looks at you, say “yes!” and give a treat. Repeat 10 times. This one skill is the base of every other cue.

  2. Add a simple cue

    Once your dog looks at you quickly, ask for something easy, like “sit.” Reward right away. Keep it fun and fast, like a game.

  3. Add a little distraction

    After a few days, practice in a new room, then the yard, then on a quiet walk. If your dog struggles, go back to an easier place for a while. That's normal.

  4. End on a win

    Always finish with something your dog does well, then stop. Ending happy keeps your dog excited for the next session.

Tips that make it stick

  • Say the cue once. Repeating “sit, sit, sit” teaches your dog that the first one doesn't count.
  • Never call your dog for something it dislikes, like a bath or nail trim. Go get it instead, so its name always means good things.
  • Use everyday moments. Ask for a sit before meals, walks and opening doors. That's free practice, all day long.
  • Be patient with teenagers. Dogs between about 6 and 18 months often “forget” things for a while. Keep practicing; it passes.

Common questions

How long until my dog listens?
Many dogs start responding to their name and “look” within a few days of short daily practice. Listening around big distractions takes longer, usually weeks of steady, patient work.
What treats work best for training?
Small, soft and smelly treats work best, about the size of a pea, so your dog can eat them quickly. Many owners also use pieces of their dog's regular food for easy cues.
Is it too late to train an older dog?
Not at all. Adult and senior dogs can learn new things at any age. Keep sessions short and gentle, and be patient with dogs that have old habits.
